= Gestures

NOTE: This documentation was generated with the assistance of AI. Please report any inaccuracies.

`GestureBitmapView` combines three detectors: Android's `GestureDetector` (scroll, fling, single tap, double tap and
long press), Android's `ScaleGestureDetector` (pinch) and the library's own `RotationGestureDetector` (two-finger
rotation). On every touch event the view feeds each enabled detector, redraws, and when the last finger is lifted
animates the bitmap back within its scale and scroll limits.

== Scroll (translation)

Dragging moves the bitmap. Three flags control which fingers can scroll:

[cols="1,1,1,3",options="header"]
|===
|`scrollEnabled` |`twoFingerScrollEnabled` |`exclusiveTwoFingerScrollEnabled` |Result

|`false` |any |any |No scrolling.
|`true` |any |`false` (default) |Scroll with one or two fingers; the number of fingers is not checked (two-finger
scroll can be combined with pinch and rotation).
|`true` |`true` (default) |`true` |Scroll with two fingers only; one-finger drags are ignored.
|`true` |`false` |`true` |Scroll with one finger only; drags with more than one finger are ignored.
|===

Defaults: `scrollEnabled = true`, `twoFingerScrollEnabled = true`, `exclusiveTwoFingerScrollEnabled = false`.

=== Scroll limits and bounce

The bitmap can be dragged past its edges by up to `leftScrollMargin`, `topScrollMargin`, `rightScrollMargin` and
`bottomScrollMargin` pixels (100 by default). When an edge of the bitmap reaches its limit the matching
`OnTopBoundReachedListener`, `OnBottomBoundReachedListener`, `OnLeftBoundReachedListener` or
`OnRightBoundReachedListener` is notified. If the bitmap is smaller than the view along an axis, scrolling along that
axis keeps it near the center. When the finger is lifted, the bitmap animates back so that it no longer exceeds its
limits, which produces a bounce effect. Set the margins to `0` to avoid any overshoot.

With `scrollTensionEnabled = true` (default) the overshoot is progressive, like a stretched rubber band. The margins
are the maximum overshoot: while the finger drags the bitmap past a limit, each outward finger movement is multiplied
by `(1 - overscroll / margin)^scrollTension` (the base kept between `0` and `1`), where `overscroll` is how far the
bitmap already is past the limit. `scrollTension` (default `1`, linear) adjusts how strong the resistance is: larger
values make it grow faster and feel stiffer, values between `0` and `1` make it softer, and `0` removes it. Movement
therefore slows down gradually and asymptotically approaches the margin instead of stopping against a
hard wall. Movement towards the limits, and movement within the limits, always follows the finger 1:1. With
`scrollTensionEnabled = false`, `scrollTension = 0` or a margin of `0`, the bitmap follows the finger 1:1 and is hard clamped at the
margin. Tension only applies while dragging: fling, `smoothScrollBy` and the bounce back animation that runs when the
finger is lifted are not affected, and the bound reached listeners keep their behavior.

Limits are computed from the area where the bitmap is currently displayed, so they follow scale and rotation changes
made in the middle of a gesture. If a pinch or a rotation leaves the bitmap beyond its scroll limits, dragging never
moves it more than the finger does: it can only be dragged back towards the limits, and the remaining excess is
corrected by the animation that runs when the fingers are lifted (or the gesture is cancelled). No animation is started
when the bitmap is already within its limits, or when no gesture changed the transformation during the touch (a plain
tap keeps a transformation set programmatically, even beyond the limits). Nothing is scrolled while no bitmap is set.

=== Fling

A fling (fast swipe) follows the same `scrollEnabled`/two-finger rules as scroll. It produces a decelerating animated
scroll that starts at the velocity of the finger: its distance is half the fling velocity multiplied by
`animationDurationMillis` (converted to seconds), still bounded by the scroll limits. `OnScrollAnimationCompletedListener` is notified when the animation ends.

== Zoom (scale)

=== Pinch

With `scaleEnabled = true` (default), pinching scales the bitmap around the focal point between the fingers. During
the gesture the scale is clamped to the range `[minScale - minScaleMargin, maxScale + maxScaleMargin]`, so the bitmap
can be zoomed slightly beyond its limits. When the fingers are lifted, a scale outside `[minScale, maxScale]` animates
back to the nearest limit. At the end of that animation:

* `OnMinScaleReachedListener` is notified if the scale is at or below `minScale`, and
  `OnMaxScaleReachedListener` if it is at or above `maxScale`;
* `OnScaleAnimationCompletedListener` is notified;
* the current rotation is kept: only the scale is animated back to `minScale`, and afterwards the translation is
  brought back within the scroll limits. `OnRotateAndTranslateAnimationCompletedListener` is not notified in this
  case.

When a double tap returns to the minimum scale (see <<Double tap>>), rotation and translation are animated back to zero
within the same animation, so the bitmap returns to its initial position in a single movement, and
`OnRotateAndTranslateAnimationCompletedListener` is also notified.

`minScale` (default `1.0`) and `maxScale` (default `10.0`) are relative to the initial display defined by
`displayType`: a scale of `1.0` means the bitmap is shown as initially fitted.

=== Double tap

With `doubleTapEnabled = true` (default), a double tap zooms in with an animation centered on the tapped point. Each
double tap adds `scaleFactorJump` (default `3.0`) to the current scale, up to `maxScale`. Once `maxScale` is reached,
the next double tap zooms back to a scale of `1.0` (kept within `[minScale, maxScale]`), resetting rotation and
translation when it reaches the minimum scale, and the cycle starts again.
A pinch gesture restarts the cycle in the zoom-in direction.

`OnDoubleTapListener` is notified on every double tap, even when `doubleTapEnabled` is `false`, so you can implement
your own double-tap behavior.

NOTE: Taps are detected by the same `GestureDetector` as scrolling, which only receives events while at least one of
`scrollEnabled`, `twoFingerScrollEnabled` or `scaleEnabled` is `true`.

== Rotation

With `rotationEnabled = true` (default), rotating two fingers rotates the bitmap around the midpoint between them.
There is no rotation limit, and the rotation is kept after the fingers are lifted (it is reset only when a double tap
returns to the minimum scale, or by `resetTransformationParameters()`). Disable it with
`rotationEnabled = false` or `app:rotationEnabled="false"`.

=== Rotation fling

With `rotationFlingEnabled = true` (default, only applies when `rotationEnabled` is `true`), when the fingers are lifted
while the bitmap is still rotating, the rotation does not stop suddenly. It continues at the angular velocity of the
fingers (at least 0.5 rad/s is required) and decelerates until it stops, over `animationDurationMillis`, around the
last pivot (the midpoint between the fingers). The fling starts when the last finger is lifted, as long as it is lifted
within 150 ms of the rotation ending (the first finger being lifted); otherwise no fling is made. This keeps small
movements of the remaining finger from cancelling it, and the rotation fling replaces any scroll fling made by that
same release. Any new touch cancels it. When it finishes, the usual limit correction
runs; if the scale is outside its limits, it is animated back to the nearest limit keeping the rotation reached by the
fling, and the translation is brought back within the scroll limits. Disable it with `rotationFlingEnabled = false` or
`app:rotationFlingEnabled="false"`.

=== Using RotationGestureDetector on its own

`RotationGestureDetector` can be reused in your own views. Feed it touch events and implement
`OnRotationGestureListener`:

[source,kotlin]
----
private val rotationDetector = RotationGestureDetector(
    object : RotationGestureDetector.OnRotationGestureListener {
        override fun onRotation(
            gestureDetector: RotationGestureDetector,
            angle: Float,
            pivotX: Float,
            pivotY: Float
        ): Boolean {
            // angle: variation in radians since the previous event
            // gestureDetector.deltaAngleStart: variation since both fingers touched down
            rotateContentBy(angle, pivotX, pivotY)
            return true
        }
    })

override fun onTouchEvent(event: MotionEvent?): Boolean {
    rotationDetector.onTouchEvent(event)
    return true
}
----

`onRotation` is called on each move event while two fingers are down, with the angle variation (radians) relative to
the previous event and the pivot at the midpoint between the fingers. `onTouchEvent` returns the value returned by the
listener for those events and `false` otherwise. The detector also exposes the finger coordinates (`initialX1`,
`initialY1`, `initialX2`, `initialY2`, `currentX1`, `currentY1`, `currentX2`, `currentY2`), the angles
(`initialAngle`, `currentAngle`, `deltaAngleStart`, `deltaAngle`) and the pivot (`pivotX`, `pivotY`). The `listener`
property can be replaced at any time.

When the rotation ends (a tracked finger is lifted or the gesture is cancelled), `onRotationEnd(gestureDetector,
angularVelocity, pivotX, pivotY)` is called once, with the smoothed angular velocity in radians per second. It reports
`0` when the gesture was cancelled or the fingers were lifted more than 100 ms after the last move. The method has an
empty default body, and the last velocity is also available through the `angularVelocity` property.

Only two fingers are tracked. Additional fingers are ignored, and when one of the tracked fingers is lifted while
others remain on the screen, tracking restarts with the remaining ones, so that no sudden rotation is reported.

== Interaction with parent views

When a second finger touches the view, it asks its parent not to intercept touch events (`requestDisallowInterceptTouchEvent(true)`), so that scrolling containers do not steal pinch and rotation gestures.

== Single tap and long press

A confirmed single tap calls `performClick()` and a long press calls `performLongClick()` (only when the view is
long-clickable and no pinch is in progress), so the standard `setOnClickListener` and `setOnLongClickListener` work
with the view.

== Animations

Double-tap zoom, the return within limits, flings and the double tap reset to the minimum scale are animated over
`animationDurationMillis` (default `200` ms) with a decelerating movement. Any running animation is cancelled when a new touch starts, when the
bitmap, `displayType` or view size changes, or when the transformation is set programmatically.
